For patients

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions women ask most often about urinary incontinence.

When should I see a doctor?

If leakage keeps happening, gets in the way of sport, work, sleep or social life, or if you have started changing your habits for fear of leaking. Seek urgent help if you have blood in your urine, fever, severe pain, cannot pass urine or develop sudden weakness in your legs.

Which specialist should I see?

Start with a general practitioner (family doctor) or a gynaecologist. If needed, they will refer you to a urologist or urogynaecologist.

Can incontinence be cured completely?

For many women, symptoms improve significantly or go away. The result depends on the type of incontinence, its cause and how regularly treatment is followed. Your doctor will help you set a realistic goal.

How long does treatment take?

The effect of pelvic floor muscle training is usually assessed after at least 3 months of regular practice. Bladder training also takes several weeks. Medicines and other options are discussed if conservative treatment has not helped enough.

How should I prepare for my appointment?

For 3 days before your visit, note when and how much you drink and urinate, and when leaks happen. Take the ICIQ-SF test and bring the printout and a list of your medicines. See How assessment works for more.

Could it be a sign of another condition?

Yes. Similar symptoms can occur with urinary tract infections, prolapse, diabetes, neurological conditions and as a side effect of medicines. That is why it is important to get assessed rather than only choosing pads.

Important

These answers are general and do not replace a consultation with a doctor.
