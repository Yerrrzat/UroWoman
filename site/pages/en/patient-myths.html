Myths and facts about urinary incontinence

Misconceptions often stop women from seeking help. Here are the most common ones.

"It's normal after childbirth and with age"

Fact: incontinence is common, but it is not normal. It is a symptom that can and should be addressed, and help works at any age.

"You should drink as little as possible"

Fact: severely restricting fluids concentrates urine, irritates the bladder and can increase urgency. Drink moderately, not minimally.

"Only surgery helps"

Fact: treatment usually starts with conservative methods — pelvic floor training, bladder training and changing habits. For many women this is enough.

"Exercises don't work"

Fact: exercises work when done with the right technique, regularly, over several months. If there is no improvement, have your technique checked by a specialist.

"Pads solve the problem"

Fact: pads help you feel more secure, but they do not treat the cause. They do not replace assessment and treatment.

"It's embarrassing to discuss with a doctor"

Fact: for a doctor this is an ordinary medical question. The more detail you give about your symptoms, the more accurate the help will be.

Where to start

Take the short ICIQ-SF test, read about the types of incontinence, and book an appointment with a GP, gynaecologist or urologist.
