Risk factors: who should pay attention

Urinary incontinence usually develops when several factors combine. Knowing your risks helps you start prevention early and notice the first symptoms.

What the study in Kazakhstan found

In a multicentre study of 2,061 women (IJERPH, 2026), urinary incontinence was independently associated with:

×2.26multiple birth
×2.08BMI of 25 kg/m² or higher
×1.83giving birth to a large baby
×1.48vaginal delivery

The figures are odds ratios: how many times higher the odds of incontinence are when the factor is present. More about the study.

Factors you can influence

  • excess body weight;
  • chronic constipation and straining;
  • smoking and chronic cough;
  • regular heavy lifting while holding your breath;
  • a lot of caffeine and fizzy drinks;
  • weak, untrained pelvic floor muscles.

Factors doctors take into account

  • pregnancy and childbirth, especially multiple births or a large baby;
  • age and menopause;
  • pelvic surgery;
  • diabetes and neurological conditions;
  • some medicines, such as diuretics;
  • incontinence in close female relatives.

What to do if you have risk factors

Having a risk factor does not mean you will develop incontinence. Start with what you can change: prevention and pelvic floor muscle training. Tell your doctor about your risks at routine appointments — especially during pregnancy and after birth.

Important

This article is not for self-diagnosis. Only a doctor can assess your risks and symptoms.
