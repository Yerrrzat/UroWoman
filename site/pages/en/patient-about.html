For patients

Urinary incontinence: where help begins

Urinary incontinence is the involuntary leakage of urine. It occurs at any age and is nothing to be ashamed of: it is a symptom you can discuss with a doctor.

What you may notice

  • leakage when coughing, laughing, running or lifting;
  • a sudden strong urge when it is hard to reach the toilet in time;
  • frequent trips to the toilet during the day or at night;
  • changing habits: avoiding sport, travel or social events.

Why it happens

Symptoms can be influenced by pregnancy and childbirth, menopause, excess body weight, chronic cough, surgery, some medicines and urinary tract conditions. The cause differs from woman to woman, so a diagnosis is never based on a single symptom.

A calm first step

Note how often leakage happens, what comes before it and how much it affects your daily life. The short ICIQ-SF questionnaire and a 3-day record of toilet visits will help you prepare for your appointment.

When to seek urgent care

Do not delay seeking medical help if you have blood in your urine, fever, severe pain, sudden weakness, cannot pass urine, or develop new symptoms after an injury.

Important

This article explains symptoms but does not replace an examination and diagnosis by a doctor.
