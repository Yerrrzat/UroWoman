For patients

Pregnancy, birth and the pelvic floor

Pregnancy and childbirth are among the main risk factors for urinary incontinence. The good news: caring for your pelvic floor during this time lowers the risk and helps you recover faster.

During pregnancy

  • Start pelvic floor muscle training — international guidelines recommend offering it to women from their first pregnancy.
  • Monitor weight gain together with your doctor.
  • Prevent constipation: fibre, fluids, movement.
  • If you already leak, tell your obstetrician — it is common and treatable.

After birth

  • Return to pelvic floor exercises gradually, once your doctor says it is safe.
  • In the first weeks, avoid lifting anything heavier than your baby and avoid straining.
  • Wait several months, and until you are symptom-free, before running, jumping or strength training.
  • Use your postnatal check-ups to ask about your bladder and pelvic floor.

When to see a doctor

Some leakage in the first weeks after birth is common. See a doctor if it does not improve over several weeks, interferes with daily life, comes with a feeling of heaviness or a bulge in the vagina, pain or fever, or appears for the first time some time after birth.

Don't suffer in silence. Leaking after childbirth is not something you just have to live with. Early help — most often exercises supervised by a specialist — gives the best results.
