For patients

Pelvic floor muscle training

Training can be part of conservative care, especially for stress symptoms. Technique matters more than the number of repetitions.

Finding the muscles

Imagine gently holding in wind. That sensation is similar to the pelvic floor muscles at work. Do not use stopping the flow of urine as a regular exercise.

A basic contraction

In a comfortable position, breathe out calmly, gently squeeze the muscles, then relax them fully. Do not pull in your stomach, squeeze your buttocks or hold your breath.

Regularity

Start with a manageable number of contractions and increase the load as advised by a specialist. The effect is usually assessed after several weeks of regular practice.

When to stop and check your technique

See a doctor or pelvic floor physiotherapist if the exercises cause pain, increase pressure in your lower abdomen, you cannot feel the muscles relax, or you are unsure which muscles are working.

An important limitation: exercises do not replace assessment if symptoms appeared suddenly or come with pain, blood in the urine or fever.
