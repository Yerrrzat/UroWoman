Clinical guidelines: working principles

Reference material for primary care. It helps structure the consultation but does not replace local protocols, clinical judgement or informed consent.

Initial assessment

  • clarify the nature, duration and triggers of symptoms and their impact on quality of life;
  • take a history: pregnancies, births, operations, medicines, neurological symptoms;
  • perform a physical examination and urinalysis as indicated;
  • use a bladder diary and validated scales where approved versions are available.

Management principles

First-line care usually includes patient education, lifestyle changes, pelvic floor muscle training and bladder training. Outcomes are reviewed at an agreed time; red-flag symptoms, diagnostic uncertainty or an inadequate response call for referral.

What to document

The leading symptom, impact on daily life, diary results, examination findings, options discussed, the agreed plan and the follow-up date. This makes the pathway clear for the patient and for the next specialist.

Based on: ICS, EAU, AUA, NICE. Check against current clinical protocols of the Ministry of Health of Kazakhstan before use.
